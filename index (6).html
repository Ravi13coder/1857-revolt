Revolt of 1857

India's first great uprising
  • 10 May 1857 — Meerut sepoys revolt. Weeks later it's a full rebellion: soldiers, peasants, artisans, deposed rulers.
  • British called it the "Sepoy Mutiny." Indians later called it India's First War of Independence.
  • Both names hold — it started in army barracks, then became something much bigger.

The causes — tap a card to reveal it

The spark

  • New Enfield rifle cartridges, greased with cow/pig fat — a direct insult to Hindu and Muslim sepoys.
  • 29 Mar 1857, Barrackpore — Mangal Pandey refuses the cartridge, attacks his officers. Hanged 8 April.
  • 10 May 1857, Meerut — jailed sepoys freed, officers killed, troops march to Delhi.
  • Next morning: Bahadur Shah Zafar proclaimed leader at the Red Fort. Mutiny becomes war.

How it unfolded

  1. 29 Mar 1857

    Mangal Pandey's uprising lights the first spark.

  2. 10 May 1857

    Meerut sepoys mutiny, march to Delhi.

  3. 11 May 1857

    Delhi falls; Bahadur Shah Zafar proclaimed leader.

  4. Jun–Jul 1857

    Revolt spreads: Kanpur, Lucknow & Awadh, Jhansi, Bihar.

  5. Sep 1857

    British retake Delhi; Zafar taken prisoner.

  6. Jun 1858

    Rani Lakshmibai dies fighting near Gwalior.

  7. Nov 1858

    Revolt crushed; Crown takes over from the Company.

Quick facts

01

Chapatis & lotus flowers passed village to village before the revolt — a mystery signal, historians think.

02

Strongest from Delhi and Meerut through Kanpur, Lucknow, Jhansi, Bareilly, Arrah — never truly all-India.

03

Punjab, Hyderabad and much of the south stayed out, or backed the British.

04

Rani Lakshmibai reportedly rode into battle with her son strapped to her back.

05

1857 remains a turning point studied across India's history classrooms.
